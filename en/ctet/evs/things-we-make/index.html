Things We Make and Do

Traditional Arts & Crafts <TopicTags tags={[EXAMS.CTET.PT, EXAMS.CTET.ET]} size="sm" />

CTET

1. Pochampally (Telangana): A traditional weaving style from Mukhtapur village. Families dye threads in boiling water with bright colors and weave them into intricate geometric patterns (Ikat). It is a dying art because silk is expensive and machine-made copies are cheaper. Weavers are leaving this profession due to low income.

2. Madhubani (Bihar): A folk art form named after the Madhubani district. It is done during festivals on walls and courtyards. The paint is made from powdered rice paste mixed with natural colors (Indigo, Turmeric, flowers). It illustrates humans, animals, and nature with no empty spaces. It is a communal activity that binds society.

3. Attar (Kannauj, UP): 'Perfume Capital of India'. Ittar is purely natural, made by distilling flowers like Rose and Jasmine in copper vessels (Deg Bhapka). It takes thousands of kilos of flowers for a small bottle. Winter Tip: Mixing Rose water, Glycerine, and Lemon juice prevents skin cracking.

4. Other Crafts:

  • Pashmina (Ladakh): World's finest wool from Changpa goats. Hand-woven, takes 250 hours. Warm as 6 sweaters.

  • Patola (Gujarat): Complex double Ikat sarees from Patan. Very expensive.

  • Warli (Maharashtra): Tribal art using white rice paste on red mud walls. Basic shapes: Circle (Sun), Triangle (Mountain).

  • Embroidery: Chikankari (Lucknow), Phulkari (Punjab), Kantha (Bengal).

Deep Dive

1. Pochampally (Telangana)

Mukhtapur village is located in the Pochampally district of Telangana (formerly in Andhra Pradesh). The traditional occupation of most families here is weaving. This craft has been passed down for generations.

  • Preparation: The process starts with bundles of thread arriving from Pochampally city. The family members—often including children—work to put the threads in boiling water to wash away dirt and stains. Then they are dyed in bright, vibrant colours using natural or chemical dyes. The dyed threads are dried in the sun and rolled into bundles.
  • The Craft: These bundles are put onto looms to be woven into cloth. The loom has many needles. The size and number of needles change according to the design. The special cloth woven here is called Pochampally. It involves intricate geometric patterns. The design is dyed into the thread before weaving (Ikat technique), which makes it very distinct.
  • Current Struggle: Silk is becoming expensive day by day. Big factories are making cheap machine-made copies of these designs. Weavers get very little money for their hard labor. Many weavers are giving up their family profession and moving to cities to work as daily wage laborers. It is a dying art that needs protection.

2. Madhubani Painting (Bihar)

This is a very old and famous form of folk art. It gets its name from the Madhubani district in Bihar.

  • Occasion: During festivals and happy occasions (like birth, marriage, or harvest), the walls of the houses and their courtyards are painted with such pictures. It is a communal activity.
  • Material: The paintings are made out of a paste of powdered rice. To this thick paste, color is mixed. This gives the painting a unique embossed texture.
  • Natural Colors: The colors used are derived directly from nature. Indigo (Neel) is used for blue, Turmeric (Haldi) for yellow, and different colors are extracted from flowers and trees. No chemical paints or synthetic dyes are used.
  • Themes: The paintings show human beings, animals, trees, flowers, birds, fish, and many other animals living in harmony. There is no empty space left in the painting; gaps are filled with flowers, birds, or geometric designs.

3. The Story of Attar (Perfume)

The Kannauj district in Uttar Pradesh is famous all over the world for its Ittar (Attar). It is the 'Perfume Capital of India'.

  • Production: Every morning, truckloads of flowers (roses, jasmine/mogra) are brought from nearby areas. Ittar is prepared from these flowers using a traditional distillation method called Deg Bhapka. The flowers are heated in copper vessels over a slow fire.
  • Products: Rose water (Gulab Jal), Kewra water, and pure Ittar are produced here. It takes thousands of kilograms of flowers to make just a small bottle of Attar.
  • Grandma's Recipe: A traditional tip for winter skin care: Mix Rose water and Glycerine in equal parts. Add a few drops of Lemon juice. Keep it in a bottle. Apply this to your hands and face in winter. Your skin will not crack or dry out.

4. Other Crafts (Match the Column)

Pashmina (Ladakh/Kashmir)

World's finest wool, woven by hand. It takes 250 hours to weave one plain shawl. It is as warm as 6 sweaters.

Patola (Gujarat)

Patan is famous for Patola sarees. It uses a very complex Double Ikat weaving technique. A single saree can cost lakhs.

Warli (Maharashtra)

Tribal art using rice paste (white) on mud/cow dung walls (reddish-brown). It uses basic shapes: circles (sun/moon), triangles (mountains), and lines. Made during weddings.

Embroideries

  • Chikankari: Lucknow (UP) - White thread on pastel cloth.

  • Kantha: West Bengal - Running stitch on old sarees to make quilts.

  • Phulkari: Punjab - Flower work embroidery.

Metals, Minerals & Fuels <TopicTags tags={[EXAMS.CTET.PT, EXAMS.CTET.ET]} size="sm" />

CTET

1. Bronze (Kansa): An ancient, strong metal used by tribals for statues and cannons (Golconda). It is an Alloy of Copper (Taamba) and Tin (Kathil). It is reddish-golden and heavy. (Trick: Bronze = Copper + Tin).

2. Brass (Pital): Another alloy, often confused with Bronze. It is made of Copper and Zinc. It is lighter and yellowish.

3. Other Metals:

  • Iron: Pure iron is soft; mixed with Carbon to make Steel (hard).

  • Aluminium (Bauxite ore): Light, rust-free. Used for aircraft and pressure cookers.

  • Gold/Silver: Malleable (sheets) and Ductile (wires).

4. Petroleum (Liquid Gold): Formed naturally from dead sea organisms. Thick, smelly, dark liquid.

  • Refining: Fractional distillation separates it into Petrol, Diesel, Kerosene, Wax, and Engine Oil.

  • By-products: Coal tar (for roads), LPG (Cooking gas), Wax (candles).

  • Conservation: It is non-renewable. "Formed in millions of years, burnt in minutes." Use public transport/CNG.

5. Bamboo (Green Gold): It is a giant Grass, not a tree. Used in Assam/Mizoram for bridges, houses, various tools, and even food (pickle). The Cheraw dance involves bamboo sticks.

Deep Dive

1. The Story of Bronze (Kansa)

Bronze is a very strong and ancient metal. It has been used by tribal people for thousands of years to make tools, weapons, and statutes. A common mistake is thinking it is a pure element like Gold or Iron. It is not.

  • Composition: Bronze is an Alloy (mixture) of two metals: Copper (Taamba) and Tin (Kathil). These metals are melted in a furnace at high temperatures and mixed in a specific ratio.
  • History: The heavy cannons found in the Golconda Fort (Hyderabad) were made of Bronze. This shows the advanced metallurgical skills of Indians in the past.
  • Usage: It is used to make statues (like the Dancing Girl of Mohenjo-Daro), medals, coins, and utensils. Bronze utensils are very heavy, durable, and have a reddish-golden color.
    • Brass (Pital): Another common alloy, often confused with Bronze. Brass is made of Copper and Zinc. It is lighter and more yellow (gold-like) in color.

2. Other Important Materials

  • Iron (Loha): Pure iron is soft. It is mixed with Carbon to make Steel, which is hard. Used in bridges, tools, and construction. In ancient times, Ironsmiths melted iron to make axes and sickles.
  • Aluminium: It is extracted from Bauxite ore. It is lightweight, strong, and does not rust easily. It is used in making airplanes (because of low weight) and pressure cookers (good conductor of heat).
  • Gold/Silver: Known for Malleability (can be beaten into thin sheets) and Ductility (can be drawn into thin wires). Used for jewelry.

3. Petroleum ("Liquid Gold")

Petrol is not made by humans in a factory. It is formed naturally deep inside the earth over millions of years from the remains of dead plants and animals that got buried under the sea.

  • Extraction: It is pumped out using pipes and machines from oil wells. The raw oil (Crude Oil) is a smelly, thick, dark-colored liquid. It contains many things mixed in it.
  • Refining: To separate these things, it is sent to a Refinery. The process is called Fractional Distillation.
  • Derivatives: From this one liquid, we get many products: Kerosene (for stoves/lamps), Diesel (for trucks/generators), Petrol (for cars), Engine Oil, and Wax (for candles/vaseline).
  • Coal Tar: The thick black residue left at the end is used for making roads (Bitumen).
  • LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas): This is the cooking gas we use at home. It also comes from petroleum.
  • CNG (Compressed Natural Gas): A cleaner fuel used in buses and autos. It produces less smoke than diesel.
  • Conservation: Petroleum is limited (Non-renewable). "It takes millions of years to form, but only a few minutes to burn." We should use it carefully. Switch off engines at red lights. Use public transport.

4. Bamboo: The Giant Grass

Bamboo is scientifically a grass, not a tree! It grows very fast. In North East India (Assam/Mizoram), it is called "Green Gold".

  • Uses: It is used for building houses (pillars), making bridges (bamboo bridges), furniture (chairs/tables), baskets, and even for cooking (bamboo shoot pickle).
  • Bamboo Dance: The Cheraw dance of Mizoram involves people dancing between moving bamboo sticks.

Space Travel & Mountaineering <TopicTags tags={[EXAMS.CTET.PT, EXAMS.CTET.ET]} size="sm" />

CTET

1. Sunita Williams (Space): Record for longest space flight by a woman (2007). In the ISS (Zero Gravity):

  • Floating: Water floats as blobs (bubbles), food packets float. She had to catch them.

  • Hair: Stood straight up (no gravity pulling it down). No need to comb.

  • View: Earth looks beautiful/blue. No lines/borders visible on land; borders are made by humans.

2. Gravity: Earth pulls everything towards itself. There is no "Up" or "Down" in space. People in Argentina stand upright just like us; they don't fall off. The sea stays in place due to gravity.

3. Bachhendri Pal (Everest): First Indian woman to climb Mt. Everest (Sagarmatha, 8848m) on 23rd May 1984.

  • Leadership: A leader walks behind, helps others carry bags, and finds resting spots.

  • Summit: Spent 43 mins on peak. It was narrow/dangerous. She bowed, hoisted the Tricolour.

  • Tools: Pitons, slings, ice axe, oxygen, sleeping bag (feathers).

4. Space Facts:

  • Neil Armstrong: First man on Moon (1969).

  • Shooting Star (Meteor): A rock from space that enters the atmosphere and catches fire due to friction. It is not a star.

  • Satellite: Man-made object for TV/Weather. Moves at constant speed.

Deep Dive

1. Sunita Williams in Space

Sunita Williams, an American astronaut of Indian origin, holds the record for the longest space flight by a woman (in 2007). She visited India and shared her amazing experiences of living in the International Space Station (ISS) where there is Zero Gravity:

  • Floating: "We could not sit at one place. We kept floating in the spaceship from one end to another. To work, we had to strap ourselves."
  • Water: "Water too does not stay at one place. It floats around as blobs (bubbles). To wash our face or hands, we had to catch these blobs in a towel."
  • Hair: "My hair always stood up! I did not need to comb it." (On Earth, gravity pulls hair down; in space, they float).
  • Food: "We had to catch the floating food packets before eating them."
  • View: She said the Earth looks beautiful and blue. There are no lines or borders visible from space. "Lines are created by us humans on maps."

2. Gravity and the Earth

Why do we stay on Earth? Because the Earth pulls everything towards itself. This pull is called Gravity.

Sunita Williams went 360 km away from Earth, where this pull is very weak, so she floated.

Is Argentina Upside Down?

In the NCERT book, children argue: "If Earth is round like a globe, don't people at the bottom fall off?"

The answer is No. The Earth acts like a giant magnet. Because of gravity, everyone stays attached to it. There is no absolute 'up' or 'down' in space. People in Argentina (which is opposite to India on the globe) stand upright just like us. The sea water also stays in place due to gravity.

3. Mountaineering: Bachhendri Pal

Mountaineering is a tough adventure. You need physical strength, willpower, and special tools. Bachhendri Pal became the first Indian woman to climb Mount Everest (and 5th woman in the world).

  • Background: She grew up in Nakuri village, Uttarakhand. She was good at studies but loved mountains.
  • The Feat: On 23rd May 1984, at 1:07 PM, she stepped onto the peak of Sagarmatha (Nepali name for Mt. Everest). The height is 8848 meters (approx 8900m).
  • On the Peak: The peak was so narrow that there was no space for two people to stand. A slip meant falling thousands of feet. She and her teammate dug into the hard snow to fix their axe and tied themselves to it for safety. She bowed her head, hoisted the Indian Tricolour, and took photos. She spent 43 minutes on top of the world.
  • Group Leader Duties: A leader walks behind the group. Help others carry bags. Let the group go ahead. Help those who cannot climb. Find a good place to stop and rest. Look after those who are not well.
  • Equipment: Pitons (pegs), Slings, Thick rope, Stick, Ice Axe, Oxygen cylinder, Sleeping Bag (filled with soft feathers for warmth).

4. Other Famous Personalities

  • Neil Armstrong: In 1969, he became the first man to walk on the Moon. Sunita Williams was only 5 years old then.
  • Kalpana Chawla: The first woman of Indian origin to go to space. She inspired thousands. Sadly, she died in the Columbia space shuttle disaster in 2003 while returning to Earth.
  • Rakesh Sharma: The first Indian to travel to space (1984). When Prime Minister Indira Gandhi asked him how India looks from space, he famously replied, "Sare Jahan Se Achha".

5. Shooting Stars & Satellites

  • Shooting Star (Meteor): It is not a star. It is a piece of rock (meteoroid) from space. When it enters the Earth's atmosphere, it travels very fast. Due to heavy friction with the air particles, it catches fire and burns up, creating a streak of light.
  • Satellite: A shiny object moving across the sky at a constant speed could be a satellite. Man-made satellites are sent into space for TV communication, telephone signals, and weather forecasting.
