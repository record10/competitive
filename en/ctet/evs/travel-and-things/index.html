Travel: Tickets, Maps & Journeys

CTET

1. Train Ticket Details (Reserved): PNR, Train No./Name, Date, Berth/Seat, Fare, Distance, Age/Sex. Note: Passenger Name is NOT on the counter ticket (it is on e-tickets).

2. Goa to Kerala Journey: Western Ghats route. 92 Tunnels and 2000 Bridges.

  • Food: Gujarat (Dhokla, Lemon Rice), Goa (Fish in Coconut oil), Kerala (Idli, Vada, Tapioca, Banana leaves).

  • Languages: Malayalam (Kerala), Konkani (Goa), Marathi (Maharashtra), Kannada (Karnataka).

  • Relationships (Malayalam): Valiyamma (Mother's elder sister), Ammuma (Mother's mother), Chittappan (Father's younger brother).

3. Abu Dhabi (UAE): Desert area. Petrol is cheaper than water (oil is abundant). Currency: Dirham. Language: Arabic. Tree: Date Palms (only tree that grows in desert). Clothes: Loose cotton to protect from heat.

** 4. Map Skills:**

  • Arabian Sea (West): Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala (Trick: GMG-KK).

  • Bay of Bengal (East): W. Bengal, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu (Trick: BOAT).

  • Formula: Speed = Distance / Time (Multiply by 5/18 to convert km/hr to m/s). **

Deep Dive

1. Reading a Rail Ticket

CTET exam often asks what information is found on a reserved train ticket. According to NCERT:

  • PNR Number: Passenger Name Record.
  • Train Number & Name: e.g., 12953 August Kranti.
  • Date of Journey: When the train starts.
  • Berth & Seat Number: e.g., A1, 22, Lower.
  • Fare: Cost of ticket in rupees.
  • Distance: Total kilometers.
  • Age & Sex: e.g., M/34.

Important Note: Passenger NAME is generally not on the counter ticket. (Although modern e-tickets have it, but for exam purposes, the answer is usually "Name is not mentioned").

2. Goa to Kerala Journey

This route runs along the Western Ghats and is considered one of the most beautiful and difficult railway routes.

  • Terrain: The train passes through 92 Tunnels and over 2000 Bridges. Why? Because the path is full of mountains (Western Ghats) and rivers.

  • States: Starting from Gujarat (Gandhidham), the train crosses Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, and finally reaches Kerala (Kozhikode/Kottayam).

  • Food on the way:

  • Gujarat (Valsad/Gandhidham): Dhokla with chutney, Lemon rice, Mithai. Batata Vada.

  • Goa (Madgaon): Fish curry (cooked in Coconut oil), Rice.

  • Kerala: Idli, Vada, Dosa (South Indian food), Tapioca. Served on banana leaves.

3. Language & Relationships

Different states speak different languages. It is important to know the "Language Map".

  • Malayalam: Spoken in Kerala.

Words: Valiyamma (Mother's elder sister/Maasi), Ammuma (Mother's Mother/Nani), Chittappan (Father's younger brother/Chacha), Kunjamma (Chacha's wife/Chachi).

  • Konkani: Spoken in Goa.
  • Marathi: Spoken in Maharashtra.
  • Kannada: Spoken in Karnataka.
  • Kuduk: Language spoken by tribal people in Jharkhand (Suryamani's story). 'Torang' means jungle.

4. Abu Dhabi (Foreign Travel)

When traveling from India to Abu Dhabi (UAE) by air:

  • Landscape: Miles and miles of sand dunes only. Mountains of sand. No trees except Date Palms.
  • Economy: Oil (petrol) is found under the sand. Hence, Petrol is cheaper than water there!
  • Currency: Dirham. (Language: Arabic).
  • Climate: Very hot. People wear loose cotton clothes and keep their heads covered to protect from the sun.

5. Map Skills

Questions often ask to identify states bordering the sea.

  • Arabian Sea (West Coast): Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala. (Trick: GMG-KK).
  • Bay of Bengal (East Coast): West Bengal, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu. (Trick: BOAT).
  • Scale: If 1 cm on map = 110 km on ground, and distance on map is 10 cm, then actual distance = 1100 km.

Formula: Speed = Distance / Time. (Remember: Convert km/hr to m/s by multiplying by 5/18 if needed).

Famous Personalities: Space & Mountains

CTET

1. Sunita Williams (Space): Record for longest space flight by a woman.

  • Zero Gravity: She floated in ISS. Water floated as blobs. Hair kept standing (no gravity). Food packets flew around.

  • Observation: Earth looks beautiful/round. "Borders are only on maps, not on Earth."

  • Dates: Neil Armstrong (Moon 1969). Kalpana Chawla (Died 2003).

2. Bachhendri Pal (Everest): First Indian woman to climb Mt. Everest (Sagarmatha, 8848m) on 23rd May 1984.

  • Training: Nehru Institute of Mountaineering. Guide: Brig. Gyan Singh.

  • Summit: Spent 43 mins. Bowed head, hoisted Tricolour.

  • Leader Duties: Carry bags for others, walk behind the group, care for sick members.

3. Karnam Malleswari (Weightlifter): From Andhra Pradesh. Father is a constable.

  • Started at age 12.

  • Can lift 130 kg.

  • Won 29 medals in international events.

Deep Dive

1. Sunita Williams (Life in Space)

Sunita Williams, an American astronaut of Indian origin, holds the record for the longest space flight by a woman (at that time).

Experiences in Zero Gravity:

  • Floating: You cannot sit at one place. You have to strap yourself to the seat. If you leave a pen, it floats.
  • Water: Water doesn't fall down. It floats as blobs (balls). To wash your face, you have to catch a blob and wet paper with it.
  • Hair: Hair always stands up! No need to comb.
  • Food: Food packets keep flying; one has to catch them to eat.
  • View: The Earth looks round and beautiful. Division lines (borders) are not visible from space, they are only on maps.

Kalpana Chawla: First woman of Indian origin to go to space. Died in the Columbia disaster (2003).

Neil Armstrong: First man to walk on the Moon in 1969. Sunita was only 5 years old then.

2. Bachhendri Pal (Mountaineering)

She is the First Indian Woman and the 5th woman in the world to reach the peak of Mount Everest (Sagarmatha). She is from Nakuri village, Garhwal (Uttarakhand).

  • Training: She was trained by Brigadier Gyan Singh at 'Nehru Institute of Mountaineering', Uttarkashi.
  • The Climb: On 23rd May 1984 at 1:07 pm, she stepped onto the peak of 8848 meters.
  • The Summit: The peak is so narrow that only one person can stand. She was shivering with cold, bowed her head, hoisted the Indian flag (Tricolour), and took photos. She spent 43 minutes on top.
  • Challenges: Sleeping bags filled with feathers (for warmth). Need for sticks to walk on snow.

3. Karnam Malleswari (Weight Lifting)

A weightlifter from Andhra Pradesh. Her father is a police constable.

  • She started lifting weights when she was 12 years old.
  • She can lift a weight of 130 kilograms.
  • She has won 29 medals in international events.
  • Her four sisters also practice weight lifting.

Things We Make: Heritage & Farming

CTET

**

  1. Traditional Arts:**

  • Madhubani (Bihar): Folk art on walls/courtyard. Made from powdered rice paste + natural colors (Indigo, Turmeric). No gaps left.

  • Pochampally (Telangana): Mukhtapur village. Silk sarees with Ikat design. Dying art.

  • Kullu: Shawls. Assam: Silk. **

** 2. Jhoom Farming (Mizoram):** Shifting Cultivation.

  • Method: Leave land fallow after a crop. Do NOT pull out bamboo/weeds; cut and burn them (Ash = Fertilizer).

  • Sowing: Land is lightly dug (not ploughed deep). Mixed cropping (Rice, Maize, Chillies).

  • Celebration: Cheraw dance (Bamboo dance) after harvest. **

3. Gregor Mendel (Austria): Monk who did experiments on Pea plants (28,000) for 7 years. Found traits come in Pairs (e.g. Tall/Short, Smooth/Rough).**

** 4. Origin of Vegetables:**

  • S. America: Tomato, Potato, Green Chilli.

  • Europe: Cabbage, Peas. Africa: Coffee, Bhindi (Okra).

  • India: Brinjal, Mango, Orange, Methi, Spinach. **

Deep Dive

1. Madhubani Painting (Mithila Art)

This is a very old form of folk art. There is a district in Bihar called Madhubani.

  • Occasion: During festivals and happy occasions, the walls of the houses and their courtyards are painted with such pictures.
  • Material: These paintings are made out of paste of powdered rice in which color is mixed.
  • Natural Colors: To make them, indigo (Neel), turmeric (Haldi), colors from flowers and trees, etc., are used. No chemical colors.
  • Design: The paintings show human beings, animals, trees, flowers, birds, fish, and many other animals. No empty space is left.

2. The World of Weaving: Pochampally

Mukhtapur village in Pochampally district (Telangana) is famous for its bright-colored silk sarees.

  • Process: Bundles of thread are put in boiling water to wash away dirt. They are dyed in bright colors, then rolled into bundles and put onto looms.
  • Heritage: It requires great skill and patience. Fathers teach this skill to sons. But now, due to low income and high silk prices, many weavers are leaving their family profession.
  • Note: Kullu is famous for Shawls, Assam for Silk, Kashmir for Pashmina/Embroidery.

3. Jhoom Farming (Shifting Cultivation)

Practiced in Mizoram and North East India. It is a very interesting way of farming.

  • Cycle: After cutting one crop, the land is left as it is for some years. Nothing is grown there.
  • Bamboo/Weeds: The bamboo or weeds which grow on that land are not pulled out. They are cut and burnt.
  • Ash as Manure: The ash makes the land fertile. While burning, care is taken so that fire does not spread to the forest.
  • Light Digging: When the land is ready for farming, it is lightly dug up, not ploughed deeply. Seeds are dropped.
  • Mixed Cropping: In one field, different types of crops like maize, vegetables, chillies, and rice are grown together.
  • Cheraw Dance: After the harvest, the community celebrates with the Cheraw dance (holding bamboo sticks).

4. What if it Finishes? (Petroleum)

Petroleum (oil) is formed naturally, but very slowly. It takes millions of years for dead plants/animals to turn into oil under the ground.

  • Extraction: It is pumped out using big machines.
  • Characteristics: It is a smelly, thick, dark-colored liquid. It contains many things mixed in it.
  • Refinery: To separate and clean these things, it is sent to a refinery. We get Petrol, Diesel, Kerosene, Engine Oil, Grease, Wax, and Coal Tar (for roads) from it.
  • LPG (Cooking gas) also comes from petroleum.
  • CNG: Used in vehicles for less pollution.

5. Gregor Mendel (The Monk with Peas)

Gregor Mendel was born in a poor farmer family in Austria (1822). He became a monk.

  • Experiments: For 7 years, he did experiments on 28,000 plants in the monastery garden. He worked on Pea plants.
  • Findings: He found that the pea plant has some traits which come in pairs. Example: Seeds are either rough or smooth. Plants are either tall or short. There is no 'medium' height plant.
  • Legacy: This laid the foundation of Genetics.

6. Origin of Vegetables (Who came from where?)

  • South America: Tomato, Potato, and Table (Green) Chilli. (Brought by Portuguese traders).
  • Europe: Cabbage and Peas.
  • Africa: Coffee beans and Bhindi (Okra).
  • Assam: Tea.
  • India (Indigenous): Mango, Orange, Banana, Methi (Fenugreek), Spinach, Brinjal, Radish.
