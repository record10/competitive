Food and Nutrition

Regional Food & Cultural Diversity

CTET

Hong Kong: Snake (Ling-hu-fen). Kashmir: Fish in Mustard Oil (Heat). Goa: Fish in Coconut Oil (Cooling).

Kerala: Boiled Tapioca + Coconut Curry. Gujarat: Sweet Dal (Jaggery), Dhokla.

Flowers: Kachnar (UP), Sahjan (Maharashtra), Banana (Kerala).

Deep Dive

1. Specific Dishes & Regions

The food habits of people depend on the climate, culture, and availability of food items in that area. For example, people in coastal areas eat more rice and fish because it is grown there. Terrain determines diet.

  • Hong Kong: Cultural preferences vary wildly. In Hong Kong, people believe snakes are healthy and warming. A dish made of snakes called Ling-hu-fen is very popular. People go to hotels specifically to eat this.
  • Kashmir: It is a cold, mountainous region. People here eat river fish cooked in Mustard Oil. Mustard oil has a heating property that helps the body stay warm in freezing temperatures.
  • Goa: A coastal state where coconuts grow in abundance and sea fish is the staple diet. Therefore, people eat fish cooked in Coconut Oil. Coconut oil has a distinct flavor and is cooling.
  • Kerala: Another coastal state in South India. People usually grow two main things in the courtyards of their houses: Tapioca (Kappa) and Coconuts. Boiled Tapioca (a starchy root vegetable like potato/sweet potato) eaten with any curry made using coconut is their favorite dish.
  • Gujarat: People here enjoy a mix of sweet and spicy flavors. They like to add a little sugar (jaggery) to their Dal. A famous dish is Dhokla with chutney and Lemon Rice. Valsad station is famous for its Batata Vada sellers.
  • Assam: The staple food is Rice. Bora rice is a common variety of glutinous (sticky) rice used here. Bihu is the harvest festival of rice crops, celebrated with community feasts (Uruka).
  • Uttar Pradesh: People enjoy a vegetable (Sabzi) made of Kachnar flowers (Bauhinia variegata).
  • Maharashtra: People make Pakoras (fritters) out of Sahjan (Drumstick) flowers.
  • Mizoram: Bamboo is central to life. Bamboo shoots are fermented and used in cooking curries and pickles.

2. Cooking Methods

Food is cooked to make it soft, tasty, and easy to digest. Cooking also kills germs. The four main methods of cooking mentioned in textbooks are Roasting (Bhunana), Boiling (Ubalna), Frying (Talna), and Baking (Sekna). Steaming is also a very healthy method used for Idlis and Momos.

3. Mid-Day Meal Scheme

In our country, many children go to school on an empty stomach. They cannot concentrate on their studies because of hunger. Recognizing this, the Supreme Court of India gave a landmark judgment.

  • The Mandate: All children in elementary schools (Classes 1 to 8) in Government and Government-aided schools must be provided with a hot, cooked lunch every day. This is their Right to Food.
  • Objectives: The scheme has three main goals: 1) To improve the nutritional status of children. 2) To increase school enrollment and attendance (children come for food and stay for study). 3) To promote social equality (children of all castes sit and eat the same food together).
  • Tithi Bhojan: A community participation program where members of the community provide food on special occasions like birthdays or festivals.

4. Community Eating (Langar)

Food brings people together. In Gurudwaras (Sikh temples), people of all religions cook and eat together in a community kitchen called Langar.

  • Seva (Service): Everyone contributes to the work. Some arrange materials like flour and pulses, some wash vegetables, some roll chapatis, some cook, and some serve water. It is a selfless service.
  • Kadhah Prasad: A holy offering (sweet pudding) distributed after Ardaas (prayer). It is made by roasting wheat flour in plenty of Pure Ghee in a huge vessel (Kadhai) and adding sugar/water. It is served hot and is considered very sacred.

Digestion & Taste

CTET

Digestion: Dr. Beaumont (Martin's stomach window, churning). ORS: Boil water + Sugar + Salt.

Anemia: Iron deficiency (Hb < 12). Eat Amla, Jaggery, Spinach.

Diseases: Malaria (Ronald Ross, Cinchona bark). Night Blindness (Vit A), Scurvy (Vit C).

Deep Dive

1. Digestion: The Journey of Food

Digestion is a complex process. It starts in the mouth itself. As we chew, our salivary glands produce saliva. This saliva mixes with the food and makes it soft. Detailed chewing helps break down starch into simple sugars—that is why plain bread or chapati starts tasting sweet if you chew it 32 times!

2. Dr. Beaumont's Stomach with a Window

In 1822, a soldier named Martin was accidently shot in the stomach. He survived, but the wound healed in a strange way—it left a hole covered by a loose flap of skin, like a washer. One could lift the flap and peep inside! Dr. William Beaumont spent 9 years doing experiments on this stomach.

Findings: Dr. Beaumont found that the stomach churns the food to digest it. The digestive juice in the stomach is acidic. He took out some liquid (juice) from the stomach and put it in a glass jar. He put equal amounts of boiled fish in the stomach and in the glass jar. The fish in the stomach digested in 2 hours, while in the glass jar it took 4 hours. This proved that our stomach churns food for faster digestion. He also found that when Martin was sad or angry, the food did not digest properly.

3. Glucose Drip for Instant Energy

When someone suffers from severe loose motions (diarrhea) and vomiting, the body loses a massive amount of water (dehydration) and salts. This makes the person very weak and dizzy. In hospitals, such patients are given a Glucose Drip. This puts glucose and water directly into the blood through a vein. Since it goes directly into the blood, it gives ** instant energy ** and strength, even without eating food.

4. ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution)

Before reaching a doctor, we can make a life-saving drink at home. Boil water and let it cool. In one glass of this water, mix one teaspoon of sugar and a pinch of salt. Taste it—it should not be saltier than your tears. Give this to the sick person sip by sip. Light food like Khichdi should be given.

5. Deficiency Diseases (Hidden Hunger)

Even if we eat full meals, we might be starving of specific nutrients. This leads to diseases.

  • Anemia (Iron Deficiency): This is very common in school children, especially girls. It causes low energy and affects studies. In the Delhi schools health check-up, thousands were found anemic.

Symptoms: Low Hemoglobin levels (Normal range is 12-16 gm/dl). Pale skin, fatigue.

Cure: Eat iron-rich foods like Jaggery (Gud), Amla (Indian Gooseberry), and Green Leafy Vegetables (Spinach/Methi). Iron tablets are also given in schools.

  • Night Blindness (Vitamin A): Inability to see clearly in dim light. Eat yellow/orange fruits like Papaya, Carrot, Mango, and Milk.
  • Scurvy (Vitamin C): Bleeding gums and slow healing of wounds. Vitamin C is found in sour (citrus) fruits like Lemon, Orange, Amla, Guava. Note: Vitamin C gets destroyed by heat, so eat these raw.
  • Rickets (Vitamin D): Bones become soft and bent. Sunlight is the best natural source of Vitamin D.

6. Malaria & Ronald Ross

For centuries, people thought Malaria was caused by "bad air" (Mal-aria). But Ronald Ross suspected mosquitoes. He caught thousands of mosquitoes, dissected their stomachs, and finally found the malaria germ in a mosquito's stomach. He confirmed that the Female Anopheles mosquito spreads it. He won the Nobel Prize in 1902. The bark of the ** Cinchona ** tree was traditionally boiled to make medicine. Now Quinine tablets are made from it.

Food Preservation & Agriculture

CTET

Home Methods: Milk (Boil), Rice (Water bowl), Dhania (Damp cloth), Onion (Dry place).

Mamidi Tandra: Andhra (Aam Papad). Ripe mangoes + Jaggery. Sun dry 4 weeks.

Pickle: Sun-dry extra moisture. Salt/Oil are preservatives. Pasteurization: Louis Pasteur.

Deep Dive

1. Spoilage vs Wastage

We must distinguish between two terms. Food Spoilage is a natural chemical process where food goes bad due to the action of microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) or enzymes. Signs include bad smell, change in color, sticky texture, or visible fuzzy growth (fungus). Such food is unsafe to eat.

Food Wastage is a human action where perfectly good, edible food is thrown into the dustbin. This happens at weddings, parties, or even at home when we take more than we can eat.

2. Conditions for Spoilage

Microorganisms need three things to grow: Moisture (Water), Warmth, and Air. Most preservation techniques aim to remove one of these factors. For example, drying removes moisture, freezing removes warmth, and vacuum packing removes air.

3. Domestic Methods of Preservation

In every Indian kitchen, grandmothers use simple scientific methods to keep food fresh for 1-2 days. These are short-term methods:

  • Milk: It is preserved by Boiling. High temperature kills the lactobacillus bacteria that turns milk sour. It is not Pasteurization (which is industrial), but domestic sterilization.
  • Cooked Rice: In summer, rice spoils quickly. To save it, put the rice bowl in a container filled with water. The water acts as an insulator, keeping the rice cool and away from heat.
  • Green Coriander (Dhania): Wrap it in a damp cloth. The evaporation from the damp cloth keeps the coriander cool and fresh (like an earthen pot cooler).
  • Onion & Garlic: They should be kept in a dry, open place (like a wire basket). If kept in closed plastic bags or damp places, they will rot or sprout.

4. Mamidi Tandra (Aam Papad)

This is a delicious way to enjoy mangoes all year round. In Andhra Pradesh, making Mamidi Tandra is a 4-week hard work.

  • Selection: The most ripe mangoes are chosen because they have the most juice and sweetness. The pulp is extracted, and fibers are removed using a muslin cloth.
  • Preservative: Crushed Jaggery (Gur) and Sugar are added to the pulp. Sugar acts as a preservative by reducing water content.
  • Process: The mixture is spread in a thin layer on a mat kept in a sunny spot. The sun's heat evaporates the water (dehydration).
  • Layering: Every day, a new layer is spread over the previous dried layer. This is repeated for 4 weeks until it becomes a 4-cm thick golden cake. It preserves the mango flavor for a year.

5. The Science of Pickling

Pickling is an age-old method of long-term preservation. Before filling pickle, the glass jars are dried thoroughly in the sun. Why? To remove every single drop of moisture. Even a little moisture can allow fungus to grow and spoil the whole jar.

Salt and Oil act as natural preservatives. When we add lots of salt, it draws out water from the bacteria cells (Hypertonic solution), killing them. Oil forms a floating layer on top, sealing the pickle from air (oxygen), so aerobic bacteria cannot survive. This ensures that pickles last for years!

6. Pasteurization (Industrial Method)

Developed by Louis Pasteur. Milk is heated to a high temperature (about 70°C) for 15-30 seconds and then suddenly cooled (chilled). This sudden change in temperature kills harmful bacteria. This milk (like Amul/Mother Dairy packets) does not spoil quickly and is safe to drink.
