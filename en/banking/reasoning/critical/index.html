Critical Reasoning

Assumptions, Arguments & Courses of Action

IBPS PO Mains SBI Clerk Mains

Rule of Thumb:

  • Assumptions: MUST be implicit. Think "Why did the speaker say this?". Do not assume extremes (Always, Never).

  • Course of Action: Solution must be practical, immediate, and should not create a bigger problem.

Strong vs Weak Arguments:

  • Strong: Based on facts, universal truths, or established experience.

  • Weak: Ambiguous, simple opinion, or simple comparison ("Other countries do this...").

Deep Dive

1. Statement & Assumptions An assumption is the unsaid premise. Example: "Computer education should be started in schools from age 5." Assumption 1: They are capable of learning at that age. (Implicit – Yes) Assumption 2: Computers are easily available. (Not necessarily implicit) Keywords to avoid: All, every, nobody, best, only. These are usually Invalid.

2. Course of Action A "Course of Action" is a step to solve a problem. Criteria for VALID Action:

  1. Solve the problem: It should directly address the core issue.
  2. Feasible: "Ban all vehicles to stop pollution" is NOT feasible.
  3. Balanced: Severe punishment for minor errors is not valid.

3. Cause & Effect Differentiating Cause and Effect is tricky. If Statement A says "Prices of vegetables increased" and B says "Farmers went on strike". Clearly, B is the Cause and A is the Effect. Sometimes, both can be effects of a common cause.

Data Sufficiency

IBPS PO Mains SBI PO Mains

The Options Pattern:

  • Option 1: Only I alone.

  • Option 2: Only II alone.

  • Option 3: Either I or II alone.

  • Option 4: Neither I nor II.

  • Option 5: Both I and II together.

Solving Process:

  • Check Statement I alone. (T/F)

  • Check Statement II alone. (T/F)

  • If both False, Check I + II combined.

  • If both True, answer is "Either-Or".

Deep Dive

1. Why Data Sufficiency is Dangerous? Because you don't need the answer. You need to know IF you can get the answer. Students often waste time solving the full puzzle. Tip: Look for "Uniques". In a direction sense question, if Statement I gives distances but no starting direction, it's insufficient alone.

2. Combine Rule Only combine statement I and II if BOTH are individually insufficient. If Statement I gives the answer, you MUST check Statement II. Why? Because if Statement II also gives the answer, the correct option is "Either I or II", not "Only I".

3. Common Topics in DS • Blood Relations: Look for gender definition. Often gender is missing. • Coding Decoding: Look for common words. • Circular Arrangement: Look for "Facing Center/Outside" info.

Statement & Conclusion

IBPS PO Mains SBI Clerk Mains

Assumptions vs Conclusions:

  • Assumption: "Before" part. What the author thought before speaking.

  • Conclusion: "After" part. What result we derive after reading.

Inference Rules:

  • Strictly derived: Must be based ONLY on given data.

  • No Reiteration: Restating the statement is not an inference.

Deep Dive

1. Difference between Inference and Conclusion An Inference is a step in reasoning, while a Conclusion is the final verdict. In exams, they are often used interchangeably. Example: "India's GDP grew by 7%." • Conclusion: India's economy is growing. (Valid) • Inference: Government policies might be working. (Probable)

2. Strong vs Weak Arguments Strong Argument: • Touches the practical aspect. • Universal truth or Established fact. Weak Argument: • Simple comparison. "Yes, USA does it so we should too." (Weak) • Ambiguous. "No, it might be bad." (Weak)

3. Cause and Effect Chains Immediate Cause vs Root Cause. • Statement A: "Huge traffic jam on Ring Road." • Statement B: "A truck overturned on Ring Road." B is the immediate cause of A. Tip: Always check if both are effects of a common cause.
