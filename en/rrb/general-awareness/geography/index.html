Topic 1 The Solar System

  • 🌑 Moon light: Reaches Earth in 1.3 seconds.

  • ☄️ Asteroid Belt: Found between Mars and Jupiter.

The Sun and Its Family

The Sun creates the gravitational pull that holds the entire solar system together. It is composed mainly of hydrogen (73%) and helium (25%). The visible surface of the Sun is called the Photosphere, while the outer layer, visible only during an eclipse, is the Corona. The temperature at the core is about 15 million°C, where nuclear fusion (hydrogen turning into helium) generates massive energy.

Classification of Planets

Planets are divided into two groups:

  • Inner Planets (Terrestrial): Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. They are made of rocks and metals and have high density. They are closer to the Sun.

  • Outer Planets (Jovian): Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. They are gas giants, made of H and He, and are very large with low density. They have rings and many moons.

Earth in Motion

Earth has two types of motions: Rotation: Spinning on its axis from West to East. Takes 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds. Causes Day and Night and tides. Revolution: Moving around the Sun in an elliptical orbit. Takes 365 days and 6 hours. Causes Seasons and variation in length of day and night. Perihelion (Jan 3): Earth is closest to the Sun. Aphelion (July 4): Earth is farthest.

Other Celestial Bodies

Asteroids: Rocky bodies orbiting the Sun, mostly found between Mars and Jupiter. Ceres is the largest. Meteoroids: Small pieces of rock. When they enter Earth's atmosphere and burn up, they are called Meteors (Shooting Stars). If they hit the ground, they are Meteorites. Comets: Icy bodies that release gas and dust when close to the Sun, forming a tail that always points away from the Sun (e.g., Halley's Comet, visible every 76 years).

The Himalayan Ranges

The Himalayas are divided into three parallel ranges from North to South:

  • Himadri (Great Himalayas): Expected average height 6000m. Contains highest peaks like Everest and Kanchenjunga. Continuous range.

  • Himachal (Lesser Himalayas): Average height 3700-4500m. Famous for hill stations like Shimla, Mussoorie, and valleys like Kashmir and Kullu.

  • Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas): Youngest range. Made of unconsolidated sediments. Valleys between Himachal and Shiwaliks are called 'Duns' (e.g., Dehradun).

Regional Divisions of Himalayas

Categorized by river valleys from West to East: Punjab Himalayas: Between Indus and Sutlej. Kumaon Himalayas: Between Sutlej and Kali. Nepal Himalayas: Between Kali and Teesta (Tallest section). Assam Himalayas: Between Teesta and Dihang (Brahmaputra).

Peninsular Plateau

The oldest landmass in India (Gondwanaland part). Divided into Central Highlands and Deccan Plateau by the Narmada river. Western Ghats (Sahyadris): Continuous wall parallel to the west coast. Higher than Eastern Ghats. Cause orographic rainfall. Major peaks: Anaimudi, Doddabetta. Eastern Ghats: Discontinuous and lower, cut by rivers like Mahanadi, Godavari. Mahendragiri is a prominent peak. The two ghats meet at the Nilgiri Hills.

Coastal Plains and Islands

Western Coast: Narrow. Konkan (Mumbai-Goa), Kannad, and Malabar (Kerala - famous for lagoons/backwaters called 'Kayals'). Eastern Coast: Wide and deltaic. Northern Circars and Coromandel Coast (TN). Islands: Andaman & Nicobar (Volcanic origin, Barren Island is active) in Bay of Bengal vs Lakshadweep (Coral origin, Atolls) in Arabian Sea. 10 Degree Channel separates Andaman from Nicobar.

Deep Dive

Himalayan vs Peninsular Rivers

Himalayan Rivers (The Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra): originate from glaciers, are perennial (flow all year), form deep gorges, and create large deltas. They are in the youthful stage and perform intense erosion. Peninsular Rivers (The Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri): are rain-fed (seasonal), have shorter and shallower courses, and flow through broad valleys. Most flow east into Bay of Bengal due to the tilt of the plateau, except Narmada and Tapi which flow west in Rift Valleys.

Drainage Patterns

Dendritic: Tree branch-like pattern (e.g., Ganga). Radial: Rivers flowing out from a central peak (e.g., Amarkantak - Narmada/Son). Trellis: Tributaries join at right angles. Antecedent Rivers: Rivers that existed before the Himalayas rose and cut through them (e.g., Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra).

Important River Projects

Bhakra Nangal: On Sutlej (Punjab/HP). Largest gravitational dam. Hirakud: On Mahanadi (Odisha). Longest dam. Tehri: On Bhagirathi (Uttarakhand). Highest dam. Sardar Sarovar: On Narmada (Gujarat). Nagarjuna Sagar: On Krishna (Telangana/AP).

Pollution and Namami Gange

The Ganga Action Plan (1985) was the first major river cleaning attempt. In 2014, 'Namami Gange' was launched as an integrated conservation mission with a budget of ₹20,000 crore to arrest pollution and rejuvenate the National River Ganga. It focuses on sewage treatment, river-front development, and biodiversity conservation (Gangetic Dolphin).

Atmosphere Layers

    1. Troposphere: Lowest layer. All weather phenomena happen here.
    1. Stratosphere: Contains Ozone Layer (Protects from UV). Jet planes fly here.
    1. Mesosphere: Coldest layer. Meteors burn up here.
    1. Ionosphere (Thermosphere): Radio waves reflected back.
    1. Exosphere: Outermost layer. Satellites orbit here.

Railway Zones (For RRB)

Northern: New Delhi Western: Mumbai (Churchgate) Central: Mumbai (CSMT) Eastern: Kolkata Southern: Chennai North-Eastern: Gorakhpur South-Central: Secunderabad South-Western: Hubli East Coast: Bhubaneswar South Coast: Visakhapatnam (Newest)

Demographics: Census 2011 Insights

Census is conducted every 10 years (started 1872, regular since 1881). Census 2011 was the 15th Census (7th after Independence). Demographic Dividend: India has one of the youngest populations, with an average age of 29 years. Urbanization: Urban population stood at 31.16%, with Goa being the most urbanized state and Himachal Pradesh the least. Child Sex Ratio (0-6 yrs): Declined to 919, the lowest since independence, which led to the 'Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao' campaign.

Structure of Atmosphere

The atmosphere is held to Earth by gravity. Troposphere: 0-18 km. Height decreases at poles. Lapse rate: Temperature drops 1°C per 165m ascent. Stratosphere: 18-50 km. Temperature increases due to Ozone absorption of UV rays. Ideal for flying. Mesosphere: 50-80 km. Coldest (-100°C). Thermosphere: 80-400 km. Contains Ionosphere (charged particles) aiding radio communication. Exosphere: 400km+. Extremely thin air, merging into space.

Composition of Air

Nitrogen (78%), Oxygen (21%), Argon (0.93%), Carbon Dioxide (0.04%). CO2, though small in percentage, is the primary Greenhouse Gas responsible for Earth's warmth and current Global Warming concerns. Water vapor is highly variable (0-4%) and crucial for weather.

Indian Railways

Railways were introduced in 1853 (Mumbai to Thane). India has the 4th largest network in the world. Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFC): To decongest the network, Eastern DFC (Ludhiana to Dankuni) and Western DFC (Dadri to JNPT) are being built. Vande Bharat Express: Indigenous semi-high speed train (Train 18). UNESCO Sites: Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, Nilgiri Mountain Railway, Kalka-Shimla Railway, and CSMT Mumbai.

Topic 5 Minerals & Crops

Leading Mineral Producers*

Deep Dive

Leading Mineral Producers

  • Coal: Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand (Jharia).

  • Iron Ore: Odisha (Mayurbhanj), Chhattisgarh (Bailadila).

  • Bauxite (Aluminium): Odisha is the largest producer.

  • Copper: Madhya Pradesh (Malanjkhand), Rajasthan (Khetri).

  • Gold: Karnataka (Kolar - KGF).

  • Diamond: Madhya Pradesh (Panna Mines).

  • Uranium: Jharkhand (Jaduguda), Andhra Pradesh (Tummalapalle).

Crop Seasons

  • Rabi (Winter): Sown Oct-Dec, Harvest Apr-Jun. (Wheat, Barley, Peas, Gram, Mustard).

  • Kharif (Monsoon): Sown Jun-Jul, Harvest Sep-Oct. (Rice, Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut).

  • Zaid (Summer): Between Rabi and Kharif. (Watermelon, Cucumber).

Agriculture in India

Nearly 54% of India's population is engaged in agriculture. Green Revolution (1960s): Led by M.S. Swaminathan. Introduced HYV seeds (Wheat & Rice), fertilizers, and irrigation. Made India self-sufficient but confined initially to Punjab, Haryana, and West UP. Rainbow Revolution: Umbrella term for all agri-revolutions (White/Milk, Blue/Fish, Yellow/Oilseeds, Golden/Horticulture).

Major Crops Conditions

Rice: High heat (>25°C), High humidity, High rainfall (>100cm). Clayey soil. West Bengal is the top producer. Wheat: Cool growing season, bright sunshine at ripening. 50-75cm rain. Well-drained loamy soil. UP is the top producer. Cotton: Tropical crop. Needs 210 frost-free days. Black soil (Regur) is best. Gujarat/Maharashtra. Tea: Tropical/Sub-tropical. Warm and moist frost-free climate throughout the year. Slopes for drainage. Assam.

Soils of India

Alluvial Soil: Most widespread (43%). Deposited by rivers. Rich in potash, poor in phosphorous. Best for agriculture. Black Soil: Volcanic origin. Moisture retentive. Self-ploughing (cracks when dry). Best for Cotton. Red Soil: Red due to iron diffusion. Found in low rainfall areas of Deccan. Laterite Soil: High temperature and heavy rain. Leaching leaves iron and aluminum. Good for Cashew, Tea, Coffee.

Mineral Belts

North-Eastern Plateau: Chota Nagpur (Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal). Richest belt (Iron, Coal, Mica). South-Western Plateau: Karnataka, Goa, TN. Rich in Iron ore and Bauxite. No coal. Petroleum: Mumbai High (Offshore) is the largest producer. Digboi (Assam) is the oldest field. Barmer (Rajasthan) is a new major onshore field.
